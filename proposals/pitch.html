10 — What Could Break This

The four things that would sink it.

We would rather you heard these from us. Each one is measured, not hypothetical, and each has a specific response rather than a promise to work harder.

Retention, not acquisition
Ten of the fifteen brands we have worked with since June have churned, averaging under two months — against an offer whose results compound over six. Acquisition is organic and costs us almost nothing. Duration is what fails.
Response: minimum terms on every new agreement (already live — three months on Leaf & Liven), and a deliberate move toward funded brands that can sustain a ramp.
The per-account floor
Reacher costs $149.75 per brand account and does not amortise the way software does. Until that changes, marginal cost bottoms out near $150 an account no matter how large we get — a services curve, not a software curve.
Response: volume pricing above thirty accounts, a push toward usage-based billing, and a self-serve tier that consumes no managed subscription. None of the three is negotiated yet.
Free only works if brands grow
Under a retainer, a flat brand still pays us. Under free plus a revenue share, a flat brand pays nothing and still costs us to serve. The brands that left us did so because they were not growing — the same population would generate almost no transaction revenue.
Response: free is a funnel and an ecosystem asset, not the revenue model. The paid tiers fund it. Brand selection is the control, and it is the single largest driver in our model.
Concentration and thin cash
Five active brands, one of them behind on payment. Across eight months of bank statements our balance never exceeded $5,046 and fell as low as $284. Losing a single client is material, and we have no buffer to absorb a bad month.
Response: this is precisely what the raise is for — the runway to widen the base before concentration forces a decision.
What we are not worried about: cost of inference. It fell 75% in a single month and held. When we model the business, inference is the smallest lever on the page — brand quality and take rate move the outcome roughly ten times as much. Cost control is what earns us the right to give the software away. It is not what makes the business worth owning.
