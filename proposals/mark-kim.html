The Problem With Commission-Only

Commission-only is not a creator strategy. It's sample distribution.

Sending samples to commission-only affiliates is functionally the same as running an Open Collab campaign — you get whoever applies, not who you need. The creators who consistently move product at this GMV range expect a fixed rate because they have optionality. Commission alone doesn't get them in the room.

⚠️

The brands that show up the most in your TikTok Shop feed aren't commission-only. They're paying fixed rates to three or four high-performers and using commissions as the second layer. The fixed rate is what gets those creators to actually produce. The commission is what keeps them motivated after the first video.

Commission-Only Model
  • Attracts creators who are testing multiple brands at once
  • No financial commitment → no creative commitment
  • Content quality is inconsistent — whoever shows up, shows up
  • Top creators pass — they're already booked by brands that pay
  • Hard to enforce brief compliance when there's no fixed arrangement
Fixed + Commission Model
  • Top creators commit — they're paid to show up
  • Brief compliance is enforceable — there's a financial arrangement
  • Higher-quality content, more consistent posting cadence
  • Commission layer keeps the incentive to convert, not just post
  • You control which creators represent the brand at the top end

We recommend a two-tier structure: a small group of top creators on fixed rates, and a broader TAP campaign with a bonus incentive structure for the volume layer. Both tiers earn commission — the fixed rate is what separates the top tier.
